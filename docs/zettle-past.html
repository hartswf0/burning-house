CARD 01/09 house-what-the-count-counts Abominable House methods

The film number fixes a casting of the index

Source

Elena Pierazzo — What Do You Do with 8 Thousand Billion Variants? — DH2025, p. 1014 and continuation. Italo Calvino — Prose and Anticombinatorics — “Order in Crime” and “Esthetic Constraints.” Abominable House — Twelve to the Twelfth.

Source Terms
loci criticicanonical versionsanticombinatoricsesthetic constraints.
Passage & Witness

PARAPHRASE Pierazzo computes 8,255,648,563,200 possible combinations of selected readings in Il Capitolo dei Frati. Canto lengths and stanza reorderings introduce further variation. Calvino’s burnt index supplies twelve deeds but leaves their order unknown. The Film Bank plays a fixed deed sequence and varies who does each deed to whom.

Research Object

What an exact count commits the project to treating as a variable.

Local Move

Pierazzo counts combinations in a model of surviving witnesses. The Film Bank counts combinations it can address and stage.

Question

Which absences in the copybook does the adaptation open to variation, and which does it settle in advance?

What Became Strange

OUR INFERENCE The same arithmetic exposes a distinction inside the project’s own count. Giving every tenant three deeds done and three suffered permits repeated pairs and yields 5,112,676,800 assignments. Calvino’s additional requirement that each tenant act once on each of the other three gives 12! = 479,001,600 assignments before applying his other aesthetic constraints.

Mechanism

Twelve fixed deed slots each take one of twelve ordered pairs. The symmetry count changes when the rule changes from equal row and column totals to exactly one use of each directed pair.

Formal Shift

<burnt index> → <fixed deed slots with variable actor–recipient pairs> → [enumerate or filter] → <addressable assignments>

Source Formalism

Pierazzo’s product over selected reading types. The Film Bank’s base-12 index. Calvino’s pair-once condition.

Our Formalization

OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX
Balanced totals allow a 4 × 4 zero-diagonal count matrix with each row and column summing to 3. Pair-once fixes every off-diagonal entry to 1. Assigning the twelve distinct pairs to the twelve slots then gives 12!.

Tension

The interface makes a precise promise about which films can be retrieved. The fiction asks a broader question about what can be reconstructed from an index.

Missing

A write-up that names the separately counted rule sets and states where deed order is fixed.

Boundary

The 12! figure isolates the pair-once condition. It leaves Calvino’s action-class rules and the project’s ledger constraints open.

Verification Test

Take a balanced assignment that repeats one ordered pair. It should pass the project’s equal-totals condition and fail pair-once.

Draft Write-Up

“The Film Bank indexes 12¹² cast assignments to a fixed sequence of twelve deeds. Its exact counts describe which assignments survive each stated constraint.”

Citation Trail

Calvino’s “Esthetic Constraints,” especially the parenthetical requirement that each tenant act on each of the others. Compare the “Three kinds of constraint” section on the project’s numbers page.

CARD 02/09 house-presence-and-witnessing Evidence in the Abominable House

Turning out the light changes who knows while leaving the cast in place

Source

Jan Niklas Jokisch and Antonio Rojas Castro — Revisiting Network Analysis in Drama — DH2025, pp. 798–799. Abominable House — Film Bank, film 1541585241617, deed IX, and ledger.js.

Source Terms
scenic copresencespeech-based presencegold standardinhabitantstrace.
Passage & Witness

PARAPHRASE Jokisch and Rojas Castro distinguish scenic copresence, speech-based interaction, and presence inferred from entrances and exits. Their proposed comparison uses fully encoded stage directions as a reference for the last of these.

Research Object

A fixed scene in which light changes the transfer of information.

Local Move

The question of what counts as an interaction becomes a question the adaptation can stage under controlled conditions.

Question

Which relationship matters for the interpretation at hand: doing the deed, being there, seeing it, or finding what it left?

What Became Strange

REPRODUCED IN CODE In deed IX of film 1541585241617, Inigo is in the bedroom and learns of the current deed. Roessler finds the curtain left by deed VIII. I reran the same assignment with the bedroom dark only during deed IX. Inigo stayed in the room, and both knowledge updates disappeared.

Mechanism

The ledger updates direct witnesses and discoveries through a light threshold. Participants can know through participation. Public slander and snooping provide further routes.

Formal Shift

<deed and room state> → <participants, inhabitants, light, surviving traces> → [apply observation rules] → <who learns what, and when>

Source Formalism

ledger.js records inhabitants and discoveries separately in each frame. The implemented threshold for both checks is light ≥ 1.

Our Formalization

NONE. The result comes from changing one lighting condition in a local copy of the published ledger.

Tension

An entrance–exit record could correctly put Inigo in the bedroom and still leave open whether he saw the deed. A knowledge graph can also link Roessler to an event she did not attend.

Missing

An explicit relation type wherever the write-up uses “witness” or “visibility.”

Boundary

The intervention establishes how the simulation behaves. It does not measure what viewers notice in the rendered film.

Verification Test

Build three records for the same beat: who was present, who saw it, and who learned of an earlier deed. Show which records change when the lamp goes out.

Draft Write-Up

“The House of Dust supplies four compositional variables. The adaptation makes them consequential by letting light and material traces govern access to a deed.”

Citation Trail

Compare the deed IX quatrain and discovery line with the frame record. For the artistic lineage, follow the four lists in Knowles and Tenney’s The House of Dust.

CARD 03/09 house-trace-attribution Evidence in the Abominable House

A curtain can disclose that something happened without disclosing who did it

Source

Federico Pianzola, Franziska Pannach, Luotong Cheng, and Xiaoyan Yang — The GOLEM Ontology for Narrative and Fiction — DH2025, pp. 201–202, “Inference module.” Abominable House — ledger.js, step and memories.

Source Terms
Attribute Assignmentprovenancesourcemethodtraceknowledge.
Passage & Witness

PARAPHRASE GOLEM models an attribution as a statement with a source and a method. One attribution can become a premise for another.

Research Object

The inference the Film Bank makes when a character finds a material trace.

Local Move

GOLEM gives evidence and attribution separate identities. The ledger stores the discovery as knowledge of the deed’s index.

Question

When should finding a trace entitle a tenant to identify an actor?

What Became Strange

OUR INFERENCE The film can say Roessler finds a curtain while the code gives her the index of deed VIII. The memory reader can use that index to look up the recorded deed and its participants. The material clue is narrower than the information the engine can retrieve from it.

Mechanism

A trace has a material and the index of its originating event. Discovery adds that index to the tenant’s knowledge list. Later code dereferences it to the event.

Formal Shift

<material trace> → <observation with source and time> → [attribute a deed or actor] → <claim a character can act on>

Source Formalism

GOLEM uses CIDOC CRM E13_Attribute_Assignment. The Film Bank records deed indices in per-tenant knowledge lists and records the material found in the frame.

Our Formalization

OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX
Preserve three assertions separately: Roessler saw a curtain, Roessler inferred a deed, Roessler attributed it to a tenant. Give each assertion its own grounds.

Tension

A fixed trace-to-event lookup keeps the simulation legible. Calvino’s investigation draws force from the possibility that a surviving object supports more than one account.

Missing

A representation of partial, uncertain, or false attribution inside the live ledger.

Boundary

This concerns how the present code stores knowledge. It leaves open how much the spectator infers from the visual clue.

Verification Test

Give Roessler the same curtain after two different possible actor assignments. Ask which claims the prop itself supports in both versions and which claims require further evidence.

Draft Write-Up

“Finding a trace currently makes its originating deed available to a tenant. This lets the simulation propagate evidence and also marks where its account of inference is strongest and most compressed.”

Citation Trail

GOLEM’s inference module and ledger.js. Read alongside the classroom film By What Light, where the difference between seeing an act and seeing its trace can carry the scene.

CARD 04/09 house-most-likely Abominable House methods

The best film found by a search depends on what that search rewards

Source

Abominable House — “The most likely story,” Film Bank, story.js, crossed.js, and published Atlas data. Kartik Chandra et al. — Storytelling as Inverse Inverse Planning — 2024.

Source Terms
believabilitylog PMonte Carlo tree searchinverse inverse planning.
Passage & Witness

PARAPHRASE The landing page presents a film selected by Monte Carlo tree search. The live engine uses the geometric mean of deed-level believability as that search’s reward and reports a separate sequence log probability. The author search rewards selected responses from a modeled watcher.

Research Object

The scope of the claim made by “most likely.”

Local Move

Separate a search result, its objective function, and the audience response it might or might not elicit.

Question

What can the featured screening demonstrate once its claim is limited to the search that produced it?

What Became Strange

REPRODUCED IN CODE The featured film 1541585241617 scored 0.59635 in the published engine. Atlas film 1974948683979, generated by the irony search, scored 0.90214 under the same scorer. The Atlas case had zero ledger contradictions.

Mechanism

Believability aggregates authored deed ratings. Sequence log probability sums the logs of normalized conditional pair scores. The browser’s MOST LIKELY button makes a seeded search with 400 iterations and sorts the films it finds by believability.

Formal Shift

<cast assignment> → <deed ratings and conditional pair probabilities> → [aggregate and rank under a stated objective] → <selected film>

Source Formalism

story.js calculates both quantities. crossed.js separately defines the watcher and the irony, suspense, and twist objectives.

Our Formalization

NONE. Both scores above were recalculated with the published scorer.

Tension

The selected film can illustrate what one search produces. Its label can also suggest an exhaustive result or a human judgment, neither of which the selection procedure supplies.

Missing

The featured render’s search seed, budget, and original score table.

Boundary

The higher Atlas score refutes a global maximum claim under the inspected scorer. It leaves the result of the featured film’s original search open.

Verification Test

Put the two film numbers in the Film Bank and record believability, log probability, and the objective that originally selected each one. Give human judgments a separate column if collected.

Draft Write-Up

“We screened one film selected by a believability-directed search. The score expresses the engine’s authored rules, and other search objectives expose different parts of the film space.”

Citation Trail

story.js, score and mcts. film.html, the MOST LIKELY handler. Chandra et al., DOI 10.1111/tops.12710, for the audience model as a design target.

CARD 05/09 house-disclosure-clock Cinematic time in the Abominable House

Two cuts can share every deed and disclose different evidence

Source

Sean A. Yeager — Postclassical Time Maps: Theory and Interpretation — DH2025, pp. 558–559. Abominable House — Film Bank, FAST / WATCH / CUT, and crossed.js.

Source Terms
fabulasyuzhetnarrative velocitymemoryconsequencewatcher.
Passage & Witness

PARAPHRASE Yeager treats presentation as the route by which the audience accesses story time. His time maps allow the same segment to be interpreted differently from different temporal viewpoints.

Research Object

A change in a cut that the current audience model cannot register.

Local Move

Apply the fabula–syuzhet distinction to two presentations of the same Film Bank assignment.

Question

When does a viewer learn the identity of an actor: from the deed, a chapter card, a memory, or a later consequence?

What Became Strange

[CODE INSPECTION] Changing the pace rebuilds the screening timeline and changes such things as chapter cards, standalone memory beats, and which consequences appear. The watcher still computes its curves from the same twelve cast assignments. It receives each deed as a complete event in the same order.

Mechanism

The renderer can repeat, foreshadow, or omit a disclosure. The watcher takes one update per ledger deed and does not consume the timed presentation segments.

Formal Shift

<ledger events> → <timed presentation segments with disclosures> → [map each disclosure to its originating event] → <time map of what the cut makes available>

Source Formalism

Yeager maps presentation time against story time. The Film Bank maintains a timed segment list and a separate deed-indexed watcher.

Our Formalization

OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX
For each clue, record when the deed occurred, when a tenant learned of it, and when the cut first gave the viewer grounds to identify it.

Tension

A curve can describe what a model infers from a complete deed sequence. A film cut can make the same sequence read differently by changing when the audience gets the evidence.

Missing

A watcher that takes the cues available in a particular cut as its input.

Boundary

Inspection establishes that the pace buttons do not change the modeled curves. Whether they change viewers’ readings remains a testable question.

Verification Test

Use the same film number in WATCH and CUT. Timestamp the first evidence for one prior deed in each. At matched checkpoints, ask viewers what they know and which cue gave it away.

Draft Write-Up

“The Film Bank can vary the disclosure of a history while keeping its deed sequence fixed. Its current watcher measures inference over the sequence of deeds.”

Citation Trail

Yeager’s Time Maps: Theory and Method, DOI 10.22148/001c.87937. Compare film.html’s build function with crossed.js’s audience function.

CARD 06/09 house-opening-representatives Abominable House methods

Two openings grouped as the same house can leave different people informed

Source

Elena Pierazzo — What Do You Do with 8 Thousand Billion Variants? — DH2025. Abominable House — OPENINGS and crossed.js.

Source Terms
average manuscriptreference copyopeningsignaturedifferent houses.
Passage & Witness

PARAPHRASE Pierazzo groups manuscript witnesses using selected textual, paratextual, or codicological features and chooses a central witness as a reference copy. The Film Bank groups three-deed openings by a signature and selects the highest-scoring prefix within each group.

Research Object

What a chosen representative lets the reader infer about other members of its group.

Local Move

Compare a representative chosen for centrality with one chosen for believability.

Question

What remains equivalent after two openings receive the same signature?

What Became Strange

REPRODUCED IN CODE The default run grouped 1,728 openings into 589 signatures. Two openings in the same group differ only in who acts in deed III: Ogiva in one, Roessler in the other, both toward Belindo. Their third-deed knowledge and room records differ. Their generated cuts stop before VII and IX, respectively.

Mechanism

The signature includes survival, body marks, dominant archetype, and current intention. It omits location and knowledge. The continuation still runs from the fuller state. The displayed representative is selected by prefix believability.

Formal Shift

<three-deed history> → <selected post-opening attributes> → [group and select] → <one displayed opening per signature>

Source Formalism

crossed.js defines the signature, the continuation, and the choice of representative. Pierazzo describes choosing the manuscript nearest the middle of a cluster.

Our Formalization

NONE. The paired result comes from the shipped default opening run.

Tension

The label “different houses” makes the grouping easy to read. The continuation can depend on details the signature deliberately omits.

Missing

A measure of how much knowledge, location, or cut position varies inside a group.

Boundary

The 589 figure counts observed signatures under the implemented rule. It does not count full ledger states or classes of interchangeable continuations.

Verification Test

Reopen both prefixes and inspect deed III. Compare the rooms, who learns what, and the next-deed distributions before judging whether one opening can stand for both.

Draft Write-Up

“We grouped openings by a compact description of the tenants. A group can contain histories with different evidence and continuations, so its representative serves as a way into the group.”

Citation Trail

In crossed.js, compare signature, agentStep, and openings. The two continued assignments are 1232343590237 and 1176469771756. Jacomy et al.’s Grounded AI Map offers a related question about keeping individual cases accessible after grouping.

CARD 07/09 house-observer-enters Calvino adaptation argument

The forecast gets the fire right and contains the intervention that follows it

Source

Italo Calvino — The Burning of the Abominable House — closing sequence. Abominable House — The House Is Occupied, script and “How It Is Made.”

Source Terms
second programmernoiseobserverparticipantforecastrescue.
Passage & Witness

PARAPHRASE Calvino’s Waldemar discovers that Skiller supplied evidence and noise to a second programmer to test whether the insurer’s involvement could be reconstructed. In The House Is Occupied, Skiller asks for an observer who stays outside. Waldemar reports the forecast and enters anyway. The fire occurs at the predicted time, and the printed image turns out to depict the rescue.

Research Object

A forecast whose evidence includes the observer becoming a participant.

Local Move

The follow-up takes Calvino’s implicated investigator from retrospective reconstruction to an intervention before the event.

Question

What does a correct prediction establish when people can act on the prediction before the event occurs?

What Became Strange

OUR INFERENCE The follow-up gives the audience a concrete reason to distinguish a correct forecast from an independent test. The forecaster’s image travels into the chain of action that the image depicts.

Mechanism

The forecast names a time and supplies an image. Waldemar reports it, crosses the yard, and enters. The later scene reveals what the earlier image depicts. The production notes state that the dot-matrix image is derived from the film’s own rescue frame.

Formal Shift

<forecast at 21:06> → <evidence received by a character> → [report and enter] → <21:17 event with an intervening observer>

Source Formalism

The production notes describe sampling the rescue frame into a dot grid for the forecast image.

Our Formalization

NONE. A causal account of the fire and a causal account of the rescue should be drawn separately if the scene is diagrammed.

Tension

The film confirms the fire and the image in the same sequence. It leaves the audience to decide what kind of success each confirmation represents.

Missing

The counterfactual history in which Waldemar stays outside.

Boundary

The screenplay establishes the sequence of disclosure, action, and rescue. It does not establish that reading the forecast caused the fire.

Verification Test

Ask two readers to identify what the forecast correctly predicts before and after the image is identified as the rescue. Record which inferences depend on the later reveal.

Draft Write-Up

“The House Is Occupied moves Calvino’s second programmer upstream. Its most consequential forecast is an image that the observer helps bring about by acting on it.”

Citation Trail

Put Calvino’s closing paragraphs beside the follow-up’s 21:06 forecast, 21:16 shutter order, and 21:17 fire.

CARD 08/09 house-film-citation Abominable House methods

A film number lets the reader recover the casting, while the cut needs its own citation

Source

Sebastian Enns, Maximilian Michel, and Andreas Kuczera — Algorithmic Edition — DH2025, pp. 231–233. Abominable House — film.html and player.html, film-number encoding.

Source Terms
Explicitly Notated CitationsUUIDhashfilm numberpacecut.
Passage & Witness

PARAPHRASE Enns and colleagues distinguish references tied to page offsets from citations that identify a passage, check its integrity, and can recover an earlier state. The Film Bank and Deed Bank use the same film number to recover the same sequence of pairs.

Research Object

The smallest reference that lets a reader inspect a particular claim about a generated film.

Local Move

Apply versioned passage citation to a film whose presentation is assembled from code.

Question

Which object is a sentence in the paper citing: a deed assignment, a ledger transition, a rendered cut, or an interaction with the interface?

What Became Strange

[CODE INSPECTION] The number survives a move between the Film Bank and the Deed Bank. Their presentations differ. The Film Bank’s n URL carries the twelve pairs, while its opening stop and pace are separate state. A link can recover the right assignment and still miss the cited beat.

Mechanism

Both banks decode the same base-12 number. The renderer separately applies a pace, builds timed segments, and may stop a generated opening before all twelve deeds play.

Formal Shift

<claim about a beat> → <film ID, deed or segment, presentation settings, code version> → [reopen the cited state] → <inspectable evidence>

Source Formalism

Enns et al. use persistent character IDs and a hash, with a versioned representation for earlier states. The Film Bank’s existing cross-links encode the assignment in n.

Our Formalization

OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX
Cite an assignment by film number. Cite a particular disclosure by film number, deed or segment, pace or named render, and revision. Include the stopping point when it matters.

Tension

A compact identifier is ideal for browsing possibilities. A critical claim needs enough information to recover the exact thing the author saw.

Missing

A public permalink that captures a complete screening state in one address.

Boundary

The proposed citation fields are a write-up convention. The deployed n parameter itself does not encode them.

Verification Test

Cite the stopped opening for assignment 1232343590237. Open the ordinary n URL in a fresh tab. Record what it recovers, then supply the stopping point separately and check that the argument can be inspected.

Draft Write-Up

“Film numbers identify cast assignments across the two banks. References to a shot, disclosure, or stopping point also specify the presentation and revision.”

Citation Trail

Enns et al., “Explicitly Notated Citations.” For the live artifact, compare the setFilm functions in the two banks.

CARD 09/09 house-gesture-as-evidence Flour-sack performance as evidence

A change in the sack’s movement can change who looks responsible

Source

Peter Broadwell et al. — The Director’s Signature — DH2025, pp. 173–175. Ugur Onal et al. — Comparing Human and AI Performance in Visual Storytelling through Creation of Comic Strips — DH2025, pp. 120–123. Abominable House — Film Bank, LEVERS.

Source Terms
pose embeddingsmoveletschoreographyexpressive cueslevers.
Passage & Witness

PARAPHRASE Broadwell and colleagues distinguish classifying performances by pose features from explaining the gestures behind the classification. Onal and colleagues show how a small change in drawn eyes can shift a reader’s inference from accidental discovery to deliberate intrusion.

Research Object

A viewer’s attribution of intention when the same deed is staged with different movement cues.

Local Move

Shift the question from whether the flour sacks look expressive to what the viewer infers from one controlled change in the performance.

Question

Which movement or lighting cue first makes viewers attribute intent to one sack?

What Became Strange

OUR INFERENCE The ledger can fix who acted and what happened while the rendered performance changes whether the act looks calculated, reluctant, or forced. The difference matters in a film about reconstructing responsibility from appearances.

Mechanism

The renderer can apply state-dependent compositing to the sack layers, including apparent size, greying, halo, and shadow. The LEVERS control lets the same assignment be viewed with or without that compositing.

Formal Shift

<same recorded deed> → <two presentations with different cues> → [compare attributions and cited moments] → <evidence about the performance>

Source Formalism

The Film Bank implements the rendering toggle. The two studies supply methods and examples for examining expressive cues. They did not study these films.

Our Formalization

NONE. Start with one shot and a matched pair of presentations.

Tension

A visual cue can communicate the ledger’s current characterization. It can also make a character look guilty before the deed gives the viewer grounds to say so.

Missing

Viewer accounts tied to particular frames or sounds.

Boundary

The toggle provides a controlled rendering comparison. A difference in how the audience reads it still needs observation.

Verification Test

Show deed XI, Snooping, from the same assignment with LEVERS on and off. Keep the crop and sound fixed. Ask viewers whether the act looks deliberate and which visible moment informed their answer.

Draft Write-Up

“The flour sacks make responsibility a question of performance. The same recorded deed can be staged with different visual cues, allowing us to test what makes an intention legible.”
