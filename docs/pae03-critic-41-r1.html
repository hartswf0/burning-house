SCION 41 · critic round 1 · PASS (narrow)

Method. I rendered /home/claude/pae03/films/41-soft-body.html headless at 1280×720. I called PAE_READY first (about 5 s to simulate), then took PAE_SEEK frames every 0.5 s across 0–32 s and every frame in the ten windows the brief names (376 frames). I built contact sheets and zoomed crops and read them with full-resolution frames, then checked 390×844 once. I judged the picture before opening PAE_CHECK. I read the source to see what is simulated and what is scripted, and probed SIM.frames for contact points, interior grain heights and penny state. Everything is in /home/claude/pae03/critic/41/ (frames/, sheets/, trace.json, check.json, probe.json).

The ten choices. Flinch (1.0): reads. At 1.04–1.17 the sack squashes, tips back and hops left about 0.26 m, and its ears flick and settle by 1.8. Flinch less (4.0): reads, smaller as asked. A squash and 6 cm of travel at 4.08–4.35. It is close to too small to see at full frame, but you can see it at full resolution. Notice (6.5): weak. At 6.75–6.80 both ears go upright and the body tilts toward the tower, which is a clear gesture. The cause is not on screen yet, though. The tower’s lean is 0.3° at 6.75 and 0.5° at 7.0, the first sifting grains show around 7.0, and the lean only becomes visible around 8.0 (2.4°). So the sack reacts before the viewer can see what it reacts to. Come, head low (9.0): reads. At 9.17 the sack pitches forward hard (head 13 cm below shoulder) and crosses in ten rhythmic squash-stretch pulses to x 0.33 by 14.0. It reads as decided. Prop (14.5): reads, narrowly. From 14.5–15.0 the sack presses into the tower’s lower left face, flattens and lifts its near ear, and the measured lean stops at 11.4° from 15.0. The stop is causal in the simulation, but the creep it stops was only about 1°/s, so on screen the prop shows as contact more than as a fall being held. The sack also presses at less than half the tower’s height. Brace (18.0): weak. The sack compresses about 6% at 18.0–18.2, but the spray from cut 3 covers it, and the sack’s centre moves 2.9 cm left (0.365 to 0.337), a small retreat the score forbids. Leap (21.4): reads well. A crouch at 21.0–21.33, liftoff about 21.5, and a stretch to nearly twice its rest height. Catch (22.4): reads. The penny settles on the top centre at 22.38–22.46 while the sack is near its apex, and it stays on through the fall. Land (23.0): reads. Contact at about 23.1–23.2 at x 0.85 in a wide squash, and the pile bursts out to both sides as a cloud. Gather (24.5): reads strongly. Grains lift off both piles in streams from 24.6. The sack rises from 0.55 to 0.94 by 28.5, the pile falls 426 → 15 → 0, and the total stays 753. Stay (30.0): reads. The knife comes down to hover about 0.3 m above the penny over 29.2–30.0 and withdraws over 31.2–31.95. The sack moves 0.0 cm.

Does it read as a flour sack. Mostly yes, but as a stiff canvas crock more than a slack sack. At rest (0–6.5, 23.5–32) the silhouette is a trapezoid, wide at the bottom, and the interior grains really do pool: about 70% sit in the lower half in every sampled frame. Two ears are always visible. Failing frames: f27–f34 (1.12–1.42), where it tips over like a tipped can rather than slumping. 9.3–14.4, where the forward lean is a rigid rotation onto the back corner, so it looks like a jar falling over, not a sack folding over its middle. f515–f536 (21.46–22.33), where it becomes an upright capsule with the weight evenly spread (lower-half share 0.48 at 22.0). This may be acceptable for a stretch, but the bottom never trails. The line of action is a straight tilt almost everywhere and never a curved spine, because the strong area constraint and the shear-only pose targets keep the outline convex. When the ears are raised (6.75–9.0, 21.5–22.5, 28.5–32) they stand straight up like horns rather than hanging as cloth corners. The ears do drag and catch up after the flinches and the landing.

Lineage. The sack is genuinely built and moved in the simulation’s terms. It is a 48-point Verlet ring with stretch, bend and area constraints, filled with 327 colliding grains. Its travel and its leap come from push-off impulses through points touching the table. Squash, rebound, ear drag and grain slosh are integrated. Flour is conserved and no grain escaped in any frame. The cards are honest about the main scripted elements: the kinematic knife, the tower’s scheduled creep, the penny flick, and the gather flow field. Three things go beyond what the cards say. First, the penny’s flick velocity is computed from the sack’s current centroid, so the world aims the penny at the sack, and the leap is aimed at x 0.82, so the catch is arranged rather than achieved. Second, once caught, the penny is pinned to a cloth edge (state 2), so it cannot fall off. The code comment calls this static friction, but it is a hard attachment. Third, grains entering the mouth are teleported a few centimetres inward. The pose “muscle” pulls toward a keyframed blend of target shapes. That is within the lineage, but it does most of the acting. In the gather, the sack stands inert while a field pumps grains into it; it does not draw them in by any action of its own.

Craft defects. The grains are 2.4 cm discs, so the tower looks like a stack of beads and the pile like pearls or popcorn, not flour. The dust is flat grey discs, and the landing puffs at 23.2–23.6 look like cauliflower. Gravity of 1.8 m/s² makes the collapse and the leap look like the Moon (grains hang in the air over 21.0–22.5). The approach slides: contact points travel continuously between pulses (for example, the contact span moves from x −0.89 to −0.39 over f250–f279 without lifting), so the travel is a skid with pulses, not steps. Measured lift is never above 2 cm before 14.4. Mobile at 390×844 is correct: no page scroll, film in the top band, controls and cards below with internal scroll. But the film is only about 220 px tall inside a black stage, so the ears and penny are hard to read at that size.

PAE_CHECK comparison. Every row HOLDS: flinch 1.08, flinch 4.08, notice 6.75, comes 9.17, props 14.71, braces 18.13, leaps 21.58, catches 22.38, lands 23.17, gathers 24.58, stays 30.0. Volume is +/-1%, flour is 753/753 with 0 escaped, and the end state is x 0.866, top 0.95, penny on top. These agree with the picture on timing. The picture is less generous on three points: notice has no visible cause, the brace includes a small retreat the check does not test, and the prop is a stop of a barely visible creep. End x is 0.866 against 0.80, which is within the check’s tolerance.

Verdict: PASS (narrow). Repair conditions, ranked: 1. Notice must have a visible cause. By 6.6 the tower should show at least 2° of lean or a visible sift stream, and the ears should rise no earlier than that (currently 0.3° at 6.75). 2. The brace must not retreat. Over 18.0–18.6 the centroid x must not drop by more than 0.5 cm (currently −2.9 cm), and the compression must stay visible: at least 10% height drop, readable on a frame not covered by spray, around 18.1–18.3. 3. During the approach (9.0–14.0), make the travel account for itself. Either the bottom leaves the table on each pulse (at least 1.5 cm lift), or the contact points stay fixed between pulses. They should not slide continuously. 4. Give the sack a curved line of action in the notice (6.75–9.0), approach (9.2–14.0) and prop (14.5–21.0) poses: a visible bend at mid-height where the top hangs over a pooled base, instead of rigid rotation onto the back corner. Through the leap (21.5–22.4), keep the base visibly heavier than the top. 5. Make the catch earned and say so. Either the penny’s flick stops reading the sack’s position and the leap aims at the penny, or the “Moved by” card states that the flick is aimed at the sack and that the caught penny is pinned to the cloth. In either case the penny should still sit on top from 22.4 to 32.0.
