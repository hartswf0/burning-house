SCION 02 · critic round 2 · PASS (narrow)

Method. I read the source only to find PAE_SEEK and PAE_PINS and to check the cards’ claims against the sack’s construction code. I rendered the film headless at 1280×720 every 0.5 s from 0 to 32 s and every frame in the ten windows (every second frame in 25.5–28.6), 408 frames in all. I read them as 14 contact sheets and looked at full-resolution frames at 0.0, 1.25, 1.42, 7.0, 8.58, 9.125, 11.0, 14.5, 15.5, 18.0, 18.08, 18.21, 21.0, 21.08, 21.5, 22.5, 23.0, 25.0, 27.0 and 28.5 s. I took pins-overlay stills at 10.0, 15.0, 21.42 and 30.4 s, and the 390×844 layout at 22.5 and 29.0 s. I wrote the judgments below from the picture before I read PAE_CHECK or round 1.

The ten choices. Flinch (1.04–1.33 s) reads. On the hit frame the sack is swapped to a squash piece and tips back hard, away from the knife. It pops up at 1.25, lands left at x −1.5 squashed at 1.33, and settles by 1.9. The ears trail the body through the hop. Flinch less (4.04–4.7) reads: the squash is smaller, the tilt is about half, and there is no travel. Notice (6.62) reads as a choice. The ears are swapped from lying flat to standing straight up, and the body straightens and tilts toward the tower. Its cause can now be seen: the tower has a visible lean of 2.1° at 7.0, and flecks sift off the cut face. The sack thinks before it acts. It holds the notice from 6.6 to 8.3, gathers itself into a wide, low anticipation squash at 8.3–8.9, and commits at 9.0. Come head low (9.0–14.0) reads. The neck and ears sit forward of and below the rear hump of the upper lobe, the line of action runs diagonally down toward the tower, and the travel happens in ten rocking steps on the up-rock. The hop lift is about 2 cm, so the steps read as a rock more than a hop, but nothing slides while held. Prop (14.5–21.0) now reads. On a held substitution at 14.5, the whole sack tips onto its tower-side corner, and its upper lobe and shoulder press into the tower’s face at about 0.41 m, with a lit hairline between them. The lean recovers from 5.0° to 4.2° within half a second, and the sack holds that pose. This is leaning its weight in, not parking. Brace (18.04) reads, modestly. The hit is a swap to a deeper squash piece (the top drops from 0.55 to 0.51 m). The sack does not retreat, the neck stays at −15° to −10°, the ears stay up, and the tower jumps to 5.0° and settles back to 4.4° against it. The brace is small on screen, but it is unambiguous. Leap (21.42) reads. There is an anticipation squash from 21.12, then a swap to the tallest stretched piece, tilted, with the ears trailing. Catch (22.42) reads: the penny drops into the neck ruffle between the ears and stays there. Land (23.0) reads. A hard held swap to the flattest piece puts the sack at x 0.80 with the penny on top, the tissue dust sits behind the card, and the sack relaxes back through softer pieces by 24.2. Gather (24.5–28.5) reads. Flecks arc from both heaps into the neck, the heaps shrink and are gone by about 28, and the sack grows in visible steps. Stay (29.9–32.0) reads. The sack is dead still, the knife descends, hovers over the penny, and withdraws at 31.2 without touching it.

Flour sack in every frame. The half-empty sack reads as a flour sack throughout 0–21.4 s, including every flinch, approach, prop and brace frame I checked. It has a flat, pooled bottom wider than the top, a tied neck with a ruffle, two corner ears, and a line of action through the pins. The 18.33–18.83 headless pose from round 1 is gone. The frames where it does not read cleanly: 22.42–22.96, where the tall stretched piece carries the round penny between two raised ears, and the silhouette reads as a small cloaked figure with a horned head more than as a sack (worst at 22.5 and 22.75). The full sack from about 27.5 to 32 reads as a bell, urn or robed figure under a penny head. It keeps its weight low and its base flared, but its straight sides and domed shoulders lose the pillowed sack shape. Neither span is a failure of the silhouette, but they are the weakest readings in the film, and they fall on the climax and the final image. In the flinch hop at 1.25–1.42 the sack’s left edge touches the frame edge; this is minor.

Lineage. The sack is built and moved in Reiniger’s language. It is five rigid cards: a bottom lobe on a base pivot, an upper lobe on a pin, the neck on a pin, and two ears pinned to the neck. In the pins overlay they stay on their pins in every frame I checked. The line of action is drawn through the pins and can be read from the outline alone. Squash, stretch and fullness are swaps from a 49×7 cut library. The code builds each piece by recomputing its outline and trimming its width to hold the area. No transform scales a piece: the only transforms are rotations and translations (rot/xf), so the claim “never a scaling” is true. The film is shot strictly on twos: no sack position or height changes on an odd frame. The two pin turns larger than 20° per exposure (6.67 s and 14.5 s) are both held substitutions on hit frames. The gather now goes through eight fill pieces (6, 12, … 48), each held 5 to 8 exposures as a batch of flecks lands. This matches the card and reads as pieces being laid in. The ears lag the neck by about 2.6 frames, as the card says. The “Moved by” card states the penny flick, and the picture shows it. That paragraph is missing its final full stop.

Craft defects. (1) The 390×844 layout breaks. The stage sizes the canvas from the half-height, so the canvas is 513 px wide in a 390 px viewport. The grid column grows to 513 px, the body has overflow hidden, and so the right quarter of the film is cut off: at 22.5 and 29.0 s the sack and penny are half off-screen. The panel is cut off too: the “Show pins” label, the thesis and the Made-of text are clipped at the right edge. There is no horizontal scroll, but content is lost. Round 1 recorded this layout as passing. (2) The penny is flicked up to 1.30 m, near the lamp, at 21.0–21.8. The card states it, but it still departs from the chronicle’s “the penny drops”. (3) The catch and full-sack silhouettes read as a figure (see above). (4) The approach steps lift only about 2 cm, so they come close to a glide at small sizes. (5) The brace is a 7% height drop; it reads, but it is the quietest beat in Act 2.

PAE_CHECK against the picture. Every row HOLDS. The onsets (1.08, 4.08, 6.67, 9.08, 14.5, 18.08, 21.5, 22.42, 23.0, 24.42, 29.92) match what I saw. Volume is 99.0–100.8% of the half reference and 100% of the full reference. The worst conservation mismatch is 1.7% of the total. The new brace, prop and gatherPieces rows describe the picture correctly: neck deviation 4.6°, ears above the lobe, line of action 19.8–20.6° into the tower, contact at 0.41 m, lean recovery 0.8°, and nine fill pieces counting the empty one, each held at least 5 exposures. Two things the check does not measure: the phone layout, and whether the catch and final silhouettes still read as a sack.

Round 1’s repair conditions. (1) Met: during 18.25–19.0 the neck stays within 5° of its 18.25 value, both ear tips stay above the bottom lobe (0.56–0.67 m against a lobe top of 0.43 m), and no pin turns more than 30° per exposure outside held hit-frame swaps. (2) Met: the line of action tilts 19.8–20.6° into the tower, the contact is at 0.41 m, and the lean recovers 0.8° within 0.5 s. (3) Met: there are eight held fill pieces, each held 5–8 exposures and swapped as a batch lands. (4) Met under its “or” clause: the penny rises to 1.30 m, but the card states the flick, the tower and its collapse chunks keep their punched holes after cut 4 (21.08), and the knife is drawn separate from them. (5) Met: the lean is 2.1° at 7.0, and the dust tissue at 23.0–24.3 sits behind the sack card.

Verdict: PASS (narrow). All ten choices read, all five round-1 conditions are met, and the construction and movement are honestly Reiniger. It stays narrow because the phone layout loses a quarter of the film and part of the panel, and because the climax and final silhouettes drift from a sack toward a figure.

Repair conditions, ranked: 1. At 390×844, the canvas’s right edge is at or before 390 px, and at t = 22.5 and 29.0 s the whole sack and penny are visible inside the top half. No panel text is clipped at the right edge: the “Show pins” label, the thesis and the Made-of paragraph are each fully visible or wrapped. 2. 22.42–22.96: the penny sits on the tied neck with at most one ear tip rising above the penny’s top, or the stretched piece shows a pooled, sack-like bottom wider than its top. Either way, the silhouette at 22.5 and 22.75 does not read as a round head between two horns. 3. 28.5–32: the full-sack piece shows corner tabs at the base and a pinched, tied neck narrower than 40% of the body width, and its sides bow outward by at least 4% of its width, so that the final image reads as a filled sack and not as a bell or urn. 4. 21.0–22.4: the penny never rises above 1.05 m (it drops from the tower top and the leap meets it), or the flick is lowered so that its apex stays below the lamp shade’s lower edge. 5. 9.0–14.0: each approach step lifts the sack’s base at least 4 cm on the up-rock exposure, so that the ten steps read as hops at phone size. The “Moved by” paragraph ends with a full stop.
