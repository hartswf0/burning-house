SCION 02 · critic round 1 · PASS (narrow)

Method. I read the source only to find PAE_SEEK and PAE_PINS and to check the cards’ claims. I rendered the film headless at 1280×720 every 0.5 s from 0 to 32 s and every frame in the ten windows (375 frames), plus 2× stills at 3.0, 15.5, 18.4, 21.0, 21.125, 22.5, 23.2, 26.0 and 30.5 s, pins-overlay stills at 1.1, 11.2, 15.5, 18.2, 18.375, 18.5 and 26.5 s, and one 390×844 layout. I judged the picture first and read PAE_CHECK afterwards.

The ten choices. Flinch (1.04–1.33 s) reads. On the hit frame the sack is swapped to a flat squash piece and tilts away from the knife. It pops tall for one exposure at 1.17, hops left (x −1.3 to −1.5) and lands squashed at 1.33, then settles by 1.9. Flinch less (4.04–4.7) reads: a smaller squash, about half the tilt, and no travel. Notice (6.62) reads as a choice. The ears are swapped from lying flat to standing up, the body straightens, and the sack tilts toward the tower. Its cause is faint, though: the tower’s lean is 0.2° at 6.67 and 0.6° at 7.0, which cannot be seen, so the only visible trigger is a few sifting flecks. Thinking before acting is clear: the notice is held for 6.6–8.3, there is an anticipation squash at 8.3–8.62, and the sack commits at 9.0. Come head low (9.0–14.0) reads. The line of action runs diagonally down to the right, the neck and ears sit in front of and below the rear hump of the upper lobe, and there are ten small rocking scoots. The hop lift is only 0.035 m (about 9 px at 1280), so it comes close to a glide, but each step’s travel happens on the rock. Prop (14.5–21.0) is weak. The sack’s right flank sits flush against the tower’s base with a lit hairline between them (15.5), the ears spread, and the tower’s lean stops at 4.5°. But the body stands nearly upright with a vertical line of action and touches only the lower third of the tower. It reads as parked against the tower, not as leaning its whole weight into it. Brace (18.04) is weak. The hit is swapped to the deepest squash piece and the sack does not retreat, which reads. Then from 18.33 to 18.83 the contact solver drives the neck to −115°. The neck and ears disappear into the tower face, the right ear points at the floor, and the sack becomes a headless double hump. At 18.92 it snaps back in one exposure. Leap (21.42–21.9) reads: anticipation squash from 21.12, then a swap to the tallest stretched piece, tilted, with the ears trailing. Catch (22.42) reads: the penny settles into the neck ruffle between the ears and stays there through the fall. The landing (23.0) reads as a hard held substitution to the flattest piece at x 0.80 with the penny still on top and the dust tissue around it, and it relaxes back through pieces 5 and 4 by 24.2. Gather (24.3–28.5) reads: flecks arc from both heaps into the neck, the heaps visibly shrink and vanish, and the sack rises to full height. Stay (29.0–32.0) reads. The sack is fully still, the knife descends to 6.4 cm above the penny, hovers, and withdraws at 31.2 without touching it.

Flour sack in every frame. The half-empty sack holds up well. The bottom lobe is flat, pooled and wider than the top, the line of action runs from the base pin through the upper-lobe and neck pins, and the tied neck with two ear corners reads as a tied sack (3.0, 11.2, 15.5). Failing frames: 18.33–18.83, where the sack is headless and the ears are rammed into the tower and floor; 21.00–21.08, where the sack’s ear sits on a tower that has gone solid black and fused with the knife into one column (a hairline remains, so this is marginal); and 23.0–23.6, where the brown dust tissue is drawn over the black card and puts tone inside the silhouette. The full sack (28.5–32) keeps weight low, with a flared base, but its straight sides, domed shoulders and the penny sitting between two raised ears read as a bell, an urn, or a small robed figure whose head is the penny, more than as a pillowed flour sack. That is not a failure, but it is the weakest reading of the sack in the film.

Lineage. The construction is in the lineage’s language, and the pins overlay confirms it. There are five rigid cards: the bottom lobe on a base pivot, the upper lobe on a pin, the neck on a pin, and the two ears pinned to the neck. They stay on their pins in every overlay frame I checked, and the line of action is drawn through the pins. Squash and stretch are true held swaps from a cut library (seven squash pieces), placed on the hit frames at 1.04, 4.04, 18.04, 21.42 and 23.0, and the motion is shot on twos. The claim “never a scaling” is literally true: no transform scales a piece. The library is generated procedurally, and its area is held by trimming the width, which matches the measured area of ±1.2%. One weakness: the gather walks through 49 fill pieces at 12 exposures a second, so the growth reads as a smooth swell, not as held substitutions. The cards also claim the ear spring lags the neck; the ears do trail by about 2.5 frames, visibly on the leap. The lit hairline stroked around the sack is a separation device more than a cut, but it does what the bar asks: the sack never merges with the tower without a readable line.

Craft defects. (1) The pin snap at 18.33–18.92 described above, the only gross error in the film. (2) The penny is thrown up to about 1.36 m (near the lamp) at 21.1–22.0 instead of falling from the tower top, which departs from “the penny drops”. (3) After cut 4 (21.0) the tower and its collapse chunks lose their punched holes and score lines, and the knife reads as part of the tower. (4) The dust tissue overlays the sack’s black card (23.0–24.3). (5) The cause of the notice is invisible (see above). Layout at 390×844: the film sits letterboxed in the top half and the controls and cards fill the bottom half, with no horizontal scroll. This passes.

PAE_CHECK against the picture. Every row HOLDS, and the measured onsets (1.08, 4.08, 6.67, 9.08, 14.5, 18.08, 21.5, 22.42, 23.0, 24.75, 29.92, 31.25) match what I saw. Volume (half 99.0–101.2%, full 100%) and conservation (3.1% worst mismatch) are plausible from the mask method. The check is wrong in two places. “Braces” passes on a height drop at 18.08 and does not see the neck swinging 100° and back at 18.33–18.92. “Props” passes on a 4 mm gap and a held lean, but cannot see that the body is not leaning in.

Verdict: PASS (narrow). Eight choices read and two are weak, the construction and movement are honestly Reiniger, and the defects are confined to known spans.

Repair conditions, ranked: 1. 18.25–19.0: the neck pin angle stays within ±35° of its 18.25 value, both ear tips stay above the top of the bottom lobe, and no pin turns more than 30° between consecutive exposures unless the change is a held substitution on a hit frame. 2. 14.5–18.0: the sack’s line of action, from the base pivot to the neck pin, tilts at least 15° into the tower, the contact point is at a height of at least 0.40 m on the tower’s cut face, and the tower’s lean visibly recovers by at least 0.5° within 0.5 s of contact. 3. 24.5–28.5: the fill goes through no more than 8 held fill pieces, each held for at least 4 exposures and each swapped as a batch of flecks arrives, so the growth reads as cut pieces being swapped in, not as a swell. 4. 21.0–22.4: the penny drops from the tower top (it never rises above 1.0 m) and the leap meets it, or the card states the flick. After cut 4 the tower and its chunks keep their punched holes, and the knife is separated from them by a lit line. 5. 6.5–7.5: the tower’s lean reaches at least 1.5° by 7.0, so that the notice at 6.62 answers something visible. The dust tissue at 23.0–24.3 is placed behind the sack card or kept off it.
